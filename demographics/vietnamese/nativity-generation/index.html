Nativity & generation

Research and synthesis by Bao Thien Ngo.

Nativity and age

In Bao’s ACS 2012 vs 2022 national cut, U.S.-born Vietnamese rose from about 37% to 44% of the population; naturalized and non-citizen shares shifted accordingly (Vietnamese American demographics).

Pew’s 2021–23 ACS analysis puts immigrants at about 60% and U.S.-born at 40%, with median ages about 50.8 (immigrants) vs 18.8 (U.S.-born). About 45% of U.S.-born Vietnamese are under 18 — the youth public is demographically a second-generation public (Pew fact sheet).

Working cohort labels (not Census categories)

Until a published Sacramento Vietnamese age pyramid exists, organizers use national nativity and age as a map, checked against local channels:

First-gen elders (roughly before ~1965) — refugee-era and later immigrants; Vietnamese-primary; temple / parish / YouTube. Gen X / 1.5 (~1965–1980) — often the last fully bilingual household generation. Millennials (~1981–1996) — bilingual remainder plus a large English-only slice. Gen Z (~1997–2012) — mostly U.S.-born, English-primary. Gen Alpha (~2013+) — English-only at home already ~half at ages 6–10.

Post-2000 arrivals sit across age labels

Migration Policy Institute figures summarized in Bao’s demographics post mark ~2000 as a useful line between earlier refugee-heavy arrivals and later family-based immigration. Recent nationals may be Millennial or Gen Z by age and still Vietnamese-primary on Zalo and Facebook. Age is not language.

Vietnamese American demographics › Language

Language

Research and synthesis by Bao Thien Ngo.

From ACS 2012 to 2022 (Bao’s national synthesis), the big move is English only: about 16% → 28%. Limited English falls about 49% → 41%; the bilingual bridge edges down about 35% → 31% even as the U.S. Vietnamese population grows.

Pew’s 2021–23 ACS cut lands in the same shape: 56% of Vietnamese ages 5+ are English proficient (~90% U.S.-born vs ~36% immigrants); about 72% speak Vietnamese at home (Pew Facts about Vietnamese in the U.S.).

Age cuts that matter for outreach

ACS 2023 PUMS age cuts (from Vietnamese heritage language use) show English-only rising sharply toward younger cohorts — roughly one in four around ages 41–45, nearly half among teens and ages 6–10.

A Người Việt Facebook group is the bilingual remainder of Millennials and older — not the English-only slice in that band, almost all of Gen Z, or half of elementary-age kids, who need English or bilingual surfaces outside Vietnamese classifieds groups.

Register, not just proficiency

ACS counts who speaks English or Vietnamese; it does not say which Vietnamese. Bao’s North American Vernacular Vietnamese (NAVV) is the U.S. diaspora register — Southern-leaning, refugee-era vocabulary, and everyday English loans (meeting, text, parking) — distinct from mainland textbook or admin Vietnamese. Useful when writing copy or designing language education for Vietnamese Americans.

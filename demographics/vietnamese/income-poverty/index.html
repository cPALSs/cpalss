Income & poverty

Research and synthesis by Bao Thien Ngo.

Household medians hide person wallets

National ACS trends in Vietnamese American demographics (2012 vs 2022) show household and family median income rising after inflation adjustment (household median about $86,025 in 2022), while per capita income remains below the all-American average. SNAP receipt stayed roughly flat (~15%); Social Security and retirement income shares rose as the population aged (65+ from ~8% to ~13% over the decade).

Do not treat the ~$86k household median as an elder’s disposable income. That figure is often an adult child’s wages plus an elder in the same unit. Festival tickets, merch, and vendor spend come out of the person’s wallet.

Poverty: children vs elders

Overall family poverty fell toward national parity by 2022. Child poverty dropped sharply and sits well below the national average; elder poverty improved only slightly and remains well above the national elder rate (~16.6% vs ~10.9% in that cut). Public assistance for elders remains structurally important.

Person income by generation (targeting)

ACS 2023 1-year PUMS person-income bands (Vietnamese alone, national, including $0) used for cPALSs channel planning put Millennials as the primary paid-product wallet (median about $51k), Gen X / 1.5 co-primary (median about $38k), and Boomer+ secondary for spend (median about $15k; about 65% have zero job earnings). About 35% of Vietnamese 65+ report an ACS disability in that cut.

Do not mix Pew’s earners-only personal earnings figure with ACS person income that includes zeros. Do not treat lumped “Gen Z 14–29” as one wallet — minors have no person-income row; ages 18–22 often median near $0 (school); ages 25–29 are a junior wallet.

Low-to-moderate income (LMI) — bank / CRA framing

Banks and credit unions often ask whether a program serves low-to-moderate income (LMI) people. Under the Community Reinvestment Act (CRA), that usually means household or family income at or below 80% of area median family income (AMI) — low is under 50% AMI; moderate is 50% to under 80%. That bar is much wider than federal poverty.

Federal poverty (FPL) in the Sacramento County table above is the deep end (~16.7% of county Vietnamese). LMI is the broader share funders mean when they say they support organizations serving LMI communities. For HUD’s Sacramento–Roseville–Arden-Arcade metro area, FY 2025 low-income (80%) limits are about $72k (1-person) to $103k (4-person); very low-income (50%) is about $45k to $64k for those sizes.

How to read LMI for Sacramento Vietnamese

On this same-vintage county benchmark, roughly half of Vietnamese households (~4,900 of ~10,300) fall under an 80%-of-county-family-median line — similar to the county overall (~47%). The sharper local signal is still poverty concentration (16.7% vs 9.3% non-Vietnamese in the ACS 2023 cut) and the place split: Elk Grove PUMAs near parity on mean income; Fruitridge / Southwest Sacramento and the Galt–Delta–rural PUMA show Vietnamese means about two-thirds of non-Vietnamese neighbors.

Cross-check with the ACS 2023 income-to-poverty bands above: about 30% of county Vietnamese are at or below 200% of FPL, and about 42% at or below 300% of FPL — the person-level near-poor band that often overlaps LMI for typical family sizes. Vietnamese family median in the SPT cut (~$81.5k) sits at about 92% of the county family median, so the community is not uniformly LMI; it is bifurcated.

For bank conversations: lead with elevated poverty and corridor gaps, then that roughly half of Vietnamese households sit under a CRA-style county LMI line. Free public festivals that draw Elk Grove + South Sacramento families (including LMI tracts) are a natural program bridge. Prefer MSA AMI when a funder uses metro limits — Sacramento County alone understates how LMI Sac County Vietnamese look relative to a Placer/El Dorado–pulled metro median.

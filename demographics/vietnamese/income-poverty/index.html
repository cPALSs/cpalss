Income & poverty

Research and synthesis by Bao Thien Ngo.

Household medians hide person wallets

National ACS trends in Vietnamese American demographics (2012 vs 2022) show household and family median income rising after inflation adjustment (household median about $86,025 in 2022), while per capita income remains below the all-American average. SNAP receipt stayed roughly flat (~15%); Social Security and retirement income shares rose as the population aged (65+ from ~8% to ~13% over the decade).

Do not treat the ~$86k household median as an elder’s disposable income. That figure is often an adult child’s wages plus an elder in the same unit. Festival tickets, merch, and vendor spend come out of the person’s wallet.

Poverty: children vs elders

Overall family poverty fell toward national parity by 2022. Child poverty dropped sharply and sits well below the national average; elder poverty improved only slightly and remains well above the national elder rate (~16.6% vs ~10.9% in that cut). Public assistance for elders remains structurally important.

Person income by generation (targeting)

ACS 2023 1-year PUMS person-income bands (Vietnamese alone, national, including $0) used for cPALSs channel planning put Millennials as the primary paid-product wallet (median about $51k), Gen X / 1.5 co-primary (median about $38k), and Boomer+ secondary for spend (median about $15k; about 65% have zero job earnings). About 35% of Vietnamese 65+ report an ACS disability in that cut.

Do not mix Pew’s earners-only personal earnings figure with ACS person income that includes zeros. Do not treat lumped “Gen Z 14–29” as one wallet — minors have no person-income row; ages 18–22 often median near $0 (school); ages 25–29 are a junior wallet.

Sacramento County

Local income and poverty detail for the county public is in Bao’s Sacramento County ACS synthesis. Prefer that post for place-specific tables; use national person-income bands when the question is generation targeting, not a local wage survey.

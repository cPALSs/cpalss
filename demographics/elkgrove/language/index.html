Elk Grove — languages spoken at home

Research and synthesis by Bao Thien Ngo / cPALSs. Figures are U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, table C16001 (language spoken at home, population age 5 and over).

Elk Grove is the venue city for the Elk Grove Lunar New Year Tết Festival at Elk Grove Regional Park. About 34.8% of residents age 5+ speak a language other than English at home (Census QuickFacts). A language spoken at home does not mean someone cannot speak English.

For partner translation decisions (for example utility or sponsor outreach materials), treat English, Vietnamese, and Spanish as the priority set for this festival’s named-Vietnamese product plus the city’s largest non-English home languages. Chinese is a strong Elk Grove nice-to-have; Hmong matters more in South Sacramento catchments than inside Elk Grove city limits.

West Elk Grove ZCTAs (venue catchment)

ZIP Code Tabulation Areas 95757 and 95758 cover much of west Elk Grove near the park. Language ranks are similar to the city: English majority, then Spanish / other Indo-European / Chinese / Tagalog / Vietnamese in the mid-single digits. Use the city table for partner briefs unless a ZIP-specific ask requires otherwise.

How cPALSs uses this for festival partners

Priority for translated partner materials: Vietnamese, English, and Spanish. Vietnamese matches the festival’s named product and a large share of the regional Vietnamese American public; Spanish is Elk Grove’s largest non-English home language; English reaches the majority of residents and most younger walk-up guests.

Nice to have: Chinese (stronger inside Elk Grove than in much of South Sacramento) and Hmong (stronger in South Sacramento — see the South Sacramento languages page). Tagalog is real in Elk Grove (~4.5%) but usually a lower priority than Vietnamese/Spanish for Tết-partner utility outreach.

Honest capacity note: cPALSs’ own public marketing is not yet running sustained Chinese or Hmong campaigns. We are still looking for a director of marketing to help build a multilingual team. Partner materials in Chinese or Hmong are welcome when the partner can produce them; they are not a promise of matching cPALSs channel spend in those languages.
